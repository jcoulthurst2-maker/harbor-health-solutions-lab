# Implementation

> Build the ability to move a solution from idea to actual use — and prove whether it changed the operation.

[← Back to Solutions Lab](../../README.md)

## What I am building

This track covers the part technical exercises often skip: **getting a system into the real operating environment.**

The goal is to understand how discovery, requirements, workflow design, testing, rollout, adoption, support, and measurement fit together — especially when the technology works but people, incentives, timing, ownership, or process still make the implementation fail.

- separating shipped work from realized value

## How the Lab trains it

Implementation missions introduce the organizational layer around the technology.

A technically sound solution may face conflicting stakeholders, incomplete requirements, a rushed launch date, low user trust, weak ownership, or an adoption problem that cannot be solved with another feature. I have to decide what must change, what has to be true before launch, and how to know whether the implementation actually worked.

## Progression

### Discovery
Understand the current state, identify the real constraint, define requirements, and make the operating problem concrete.

### Readiness
Plan UAT, dependencies, ownership, training, launch criteria, and failure handling before go-live.

### Go-live
Manage cutover, issue escalation, communication, support, and the first signals of adoption or failure.

### Value realization
Measure what changed after launch, distinguish activity from outcome, and decide what should be corrected, expanded, or stopped.

## What counts as proof

Completed evidence can include:

- discovery notes and workflow maps
- requirements and acceptance criteria
- UAT plans and issue logs
- readiness checklists
- rollout and cutover plans
- training materials
- adoption measures
- post-launch reviews
- decision briefs and lessons learned
