# AI Systems

> Build the ability to design AI that is useful because it understands context, behaves predictably, and improves a real decision.

[← Back to Solutions Lab](../../README.md)

## What I am building

This track is about moving beyond model demos into **complete AI systems**.

The goal is to understand how models behave inside workflows: what context they need, how tools and retrieval should work, how state is carried, how outputs are evaluated, where humans retain authority, and what failure looks like when the system is wrong.

## Core capabilities

- model behavior and limitations
- prompt and context design
- retrieval and grounding
- tool use and orchestration
- state and continuity
- evaluation design
- structured outputs
- uncertainty and abstention
- guardrails and authority boundaries
- human review patterns
- latency and cost tradeoffs
- failure modes and recovery
- measuring whether AI changes an outcome rather than merely producing output

## How the Lab trains it

AI missions will begin with an operating problem, not with an instruction to "add AI."

I have to decide whether intelligence belongs in the workflow at all, what information it should see, what actions it may prepare or perform, how its behavior will be tested, and when it should stay out of the way.

Some missions may reward automation. Others may reveal that the better system is a smaller model, a rules layer, stronger data, a different interface, or no AI intervention at all.

## Progression

### Foundation
Understand model behavior, context limits, structured prompting, and common failure modes.

### Grounded systems
Use retrieval, tools, structured data, and validation to make model behavior more useful and inspectable.

### Evaluation and authority
Build tests around quality, uncertainty, failure, escalation, and the boundary between recommendation and action.

### Persistent intelligence
Reason about continuity, state, changing context, restraint, and systems that become more useful across time rather than restarting every interaction.

## What counts as proof

Completed evidence can include:

- evaluation sets and scorecards
- model/system comparisons
- retrieval experiments
- tool-use workflows
- structured-output tests
- failure analyses
- authority and escalation designs
- architecture notes
- mission postmortems
- evidence that the AI changed a useful outcome
