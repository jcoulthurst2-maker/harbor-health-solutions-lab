# Why These Healthcare Problems

## What this page is for

Harbor Health is fictional, but the pressures behind it are real. This page explains **why these are worthwhile healthcare problems to practice solving in 2026**.

It is not a second roadmap and does not explain the technical curriculum.

## 2026 national context

**Prior authorization** — in May 2026, the AMA reported physicians average **40 prior authorizations per week**, consuming about **13 hours of physician and staff time**. The same survey found **95%** said prior authorization delays necessary care and **94%** said it contributes to burnout. [Source: AMA, May 2026](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/only-1-3-doctors-trusts-insurers-prior-authorization)

**Interoperability** — an ASTP/ONC update published in February 2026 found **76% of hospitals** participated in all four measured interoperability domains in 2025, showing broad progress but not universal exchange. [Source: ASTP/ONC, February 2026](https://healthit.gov/data/quickstats/electronic-health-information-exchange-hospitals/)

**Administrative data exchange** — a February 2026 ASTP/ONC brief found about **9 in 10 hospitals** enabled API-based patient access, but only about **1 in 3** used standards-based APIs for at least one administrative data-sharing purpose. [Source: ASTP/ONC, February 2026](https://healthit.gov/data/data-briefs/hospital-use-of-apis-to-enable-data-sharing-between-ehrs-and-third-party-technology/)

**Electronic prior authorization** — CMS requires impacted payers to publicly report prior-authorization metrics beginning in **2026**, while major Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and Prior Authorization API requirements begin in **2027**. CMS also proposed additional FHIR-based prior-authorization requirements for drugs in April 2026. [Source: CMS, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/burden-reduction/overview/interoperability/frequently-asked-questions/prior-authorization-api) · [2026 proposed rule](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-cms-interoperability-standards-prior-authorization-drugs-proposed-rule)

**Cybersecurity & continuity** — in April 2026, HHS announced four ransomware-related HIPAA settlements involving breaches affecting more than **427,000 people**. By July 2026, OCR announced its **21st ransomware enforcement action**. [Source: HHS, April 2026](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-four-ransomware-investigations.html) · [HHS, July 2026](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-ocr-settles-ransomware-investigation-with-healthcare-system.html)

## Why Harbor Health uses these pressures

These conditions create the kind of work I want the lab to force me to solve: unclear root causes, fragmented systems, manual burden, unreliable information, implementation risk, security constraints, and decisions that affect both staff and patients.

The problems are chosen because they require more than one kind of thinking at a time. A strong answer may require operational judgment, technical evidence, workflow redesign, architecture, implementation planning, or a decision **not** to add more technology.

## The standard

A Harbor Health problem belongs in the lab only if it could plausibly matter to a real U.S. healthcare organization and force a meaningful decision.

The technical work should exist because the healthcare problem demands it—not because the curriculum needs another exercise.
