# Why These Problems

Harbor Health is designed around healthcare problems that force operations, data, technology, and implementation to work together.

These are not random training scenarios. They reflect pressures U.S. healthcare organizations are dealing with now.

## The national context

- **Access pressure is structural.** The AAMC projects the United States could face a shortage of up to **86,000 physicians by 2036**, increasing pressure on timely access and capacity. [Source: AAMC](https://www.aamc.org/news/press-releases/new-aamc-report-shows-continuing-projected-physician-shortage)
- **Prior authorization consumes major clinical capacity.** In the AMA's 2025 physician survey, practices reported an average of **40 prior authorizations per physician each week** and about **13 hours of physician and staff time** spent on them. [Source: AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-survey-prior-authorization-reform-pledge-falls-short-physicians)
- **Interoperability has improved, but it is not finished.** In 2025, **76% of hospitals** reported participating in all four measured interoperability domains. [Source: ASTP/ONC](https://healthit.gov/data/quickstats/electronic-health-information-exchange-hospitals/)
- **Hospitals use APIs widely, but administrative exchange is still fragmented.** In 2024, about **9 in 10 hospitals** enabled patient access through APIs, while only about **one-third** reported standards-based API exchange for at least one administrative use case. [Source: ASTP/ONC](https://healthit.gov/data/data-briefs/hospital-use-of-apis-to-enable-data-sharing-between-ehrs-and-third-party-technology/)
- **The federal direction is toward more structured electronic exchange.** CMS requires impacted payers to implement Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and Prior Authorization APIs, with major API requirements beginning in **2027**. [Source: CMS](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/burden-reduction/overview/interoperability/policies-regulations/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f)

Harbor Health turns those national pressures into one fictional hospital where I can practice solving the operational and technical problems behind them.

## What the lab works on

- **Appointment access** — proves whether the real issue is scheduling, staffing, referrals, capacity, or data.
- **Data trust** — prevents dashboards, automation, and AI from confidently using bad information.
- **Prior authorization** — combines payer rules, documentation, manual work, technology, and human judgment.
- **Fragmented systems** — reflects how healthcare work moves across EHRs, vendor tools, spreadsheets, portals, and internal systems.
- **FHIR / HL7** — teaches not just how systems connect, but how healthcare information keeps consistent meaning.
- **AI** — teaches when AI adds value, when it does not, and how to keep people in control.
- **Security and continuity** — makes failure, permissions, logging, recovery, and safe operation part of the design.
- **Implementation and adoption** — recognizes that a technically correct system can still fail if people will not use it.

## Design rule

A problem is not introduced because it is “time to learn Python” or “time to learn AWS.”

It appears because Harbor Health has a believable healthcare problem, and the technology is introduced because solving that problem requires it.
