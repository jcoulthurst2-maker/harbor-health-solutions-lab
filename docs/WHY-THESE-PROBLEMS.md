# Why These Problems

Harbor Health is designed around healthcare problems that force operations, data, technology, and implementation to work together.

These are not random training scenarios. They reflect pressures U.S. healthcare organizations are dealing with now.

## 2026 national context

- **Prior authorization is still consuming major clinical capacity.** In May 2026, the AMA reported that physicians average **40 prior authorizations per week**, consuming about **13 hours of physician and staff time**. The same survey found **95%** of physicians said prior authorization delays necessary care and **94%** said it contributes to burnout. [Source: AMA, May 2026](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/only-1-3-doctors-trusts-insurers-prior-authorization)
- **Interoperability is improving, but it is not finished.** An ASTP/ONC update published in February 2026 found **76% of hospitals** participated in all four measured interoperability domains in 2025. [Source: ASTP/ONC, February 2026](https://healthit.gov/data/quickstats/electronic-health-information-exchange-hospitals/)
- **Standards-based administrative exchange still has a large gap.** A February 2026 ASTP/ONC data brief found about **9 in 10 hospitals** enabled API-based patient access, but only about **1 in 3** used standards-based APIs for at least one administrative data-sharing purpose. [Source: ASTP/ONC, February 2026](https://healthit.gov/data/data-briefs/hospital-use-of-apis-to-enable-data-sharing-between-ehrs-and-third-party-technology/)
- **The federal push toward electronic prior authorization is active now.** CMS requires impacted payers to publicly report prior-authorization metrics beginning in **2026**, while major Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and Prior Authorization API requirements begin in **2027**. CMS also proposed additional FHIR-based prior-authorization requirements for drugs in April 2026. [Source: CMS, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/burden-reduction/overview/interoperability/frequently-asked-questions/prior-authorization-api) · [2026 proposed rule](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-cms-interoperability-standards-prior-authorization-drugs-proposed-rule)
- **Cybersecurity remains an operating problem, not just an IT problem.** In April 2026, HHS announced four ransomware-related HIPAA settlements involving breaches that affected more than **427,000 people**. By July 2026, OCR announced its **21st ransomware enforcement action**. [Source: HHS, April 2026](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-four-ransomware-investigations.html) · [HHS, July 2026](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-ocr-settles-ransomware-investigation-with-healthcare-system.html)

Harbor Health turns those real national pressures into one fictional hospital where I can practice solving the operational and technical problems behind them end to end.

## What the lab works on

- **Appointment access** — proves whether the real issue is scheduling, staffing, referrals, capacity, or data.
- **Data trust** — prevents dashboards, automation, and AI from confidently using bad information.
- **Prior authorization** — combines payer rules, documentation, manual work, technology, and human judgment.
- **Fragmented systems** — reflects how healthcare work moves across EHRs, vendor tools, spreadsheets, portals, and internal systems.
- **FHIR / HL7** — teaches not just how systems connect, but how healthcare information keeps consistent meaning.
- **AI** — teaches when AI adds value, when it does not, and how to keep people in control.
- **Security and continuity** — makes failure, permissions, logging, recovery, and safe operation part of the design.
- **Implementation and adoption** — recognizes that a technically correct system can still fail if people will not use it.

## Design rule

A problem is not introduced because it is “time to learn Python” or “time to learn AWS.”

It appears because Harbor Health has a believable healthcare problem, and the technology is introduced because solving that problem requires it.
