# Implementation & Adoption

Harbor Health is not finished when the technology works.

A complete solution has to move into real use inside a healthcare organization.

## What that means here

For serious missions, I also work through:

- **Stakeholders** — who uses, owns, approves, supports, or could block the change.
- **Current state** — how the work happens today and where the handoffs, delays, workarounds, and risks live.
- **Future state** — what the workflow should look like after the solution is introduced.
- **Requirements** — what Operations, clinicians, technical teams, compliance, and leadership need from the solution.
- **User acceptance testing** — realistic users testing realistic scenarios before broad launch.
- **Pilot and rollout** — deciding where to start, what has to work first, and when it is safe to expand.
- **Training and enablement** — what staff need to know, when they should trust the new workflow, and what to do when something looks wrong.
- **Adoption risk** — why people might resist, ignore, misuse, or work around the solution.
- **Ownership and support** — who is responsible after launch and how frontline feedback reaches the teams that can act on it.
- **Measurement** — operational results, usage, quality, reliability, staff feedback, and business or patient outcomes.
- **Post-launch improvement** — what should change after the first real-world results come back.

## Why it matters

This is what makes the project broader than a coding or architecture lab.

The technical side asks: **Can I design and prove the right solution?**

The implementation side asks: **Can I get the organization to use it successfully?**

A complete Harbor Health mission should be able to answer both.
