# Client Solutions, Implementation, Go-Live & Adoption

## What this proves

I can take a technically sound healthcare solution and move it into real organizational use.

## Why it matters

A working product can still fail if the problem was misunderstood, requirements were weak, users were not prepared, ownership is unclear, or the organization cannot tell whether the change created value.

Harbor Health treats implementation as part of the solution itself, not as work that begins after the build is finished.

## How the work moves

**Discover & align** — define the problem, stakeholders, current workflow, requirements, expected value, and decision owners.

**Prepare & validate** — design the future workflow, confirm acceptance criteria, run user acceptance testing, test failure scenarios, and decide whether the solution is ready for a pilot or launch.

**Launch & support** — plan rollout, training, communications, support paths, escalation, monitoring, and go-live readiness.

**Adopt & improve** — measure usage, operational results, quality, reliability, staff feedback, and whether the original value promise was achieved.

## How this matures across the 32 weeks

**SQL / Assess** — discovery, stakeholders, current-state workflow, ownership, baselines, and early requirements.

**Python / Design & Build** — future-state workflow, prototypes, acceptance criteria, value hypothesis, and early UAT scenarios.

**APIs / Build & Integrate** — dependencies, system ownership, handoffs, end-to-end testing, delivery handoff, and pilot design.

**FHIR / HL7 / Validate** — realistic UAT, healthcare workflow validation, training, adoption risks, operational readiness, and rollout decisions.

**AWS & architecture / Go Live, Adopt & Measure** — security and readiness, monitoring, launch, support model, adoption, KPI tracking, post-launch review, and scale decisions.

## Evidence this produces

Depending on the problem, the public record can include discovery findings, stakeholder maps, requirements, workflow designs, UAT plans and results, pilot plans, launch-readiness decisions, training plans, support models, adoption measures, KPI plans, and post-launch reviews.

## What good looks like

The solution is complete only when I can explain who needs it, what changes, how it is tested, what has to be true for go-live, who owns it after launch, whether people actually use it, and how leadership will know it worked.
