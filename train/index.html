26-WEEK OPERATING MAP

Problems drive the curriculum.

The Lab no longer teaches SQL, Python, APIs, cloud, AI, security, or architecture as disconnected subjects. Each capability appears when an engagement requires it, then reappears later under less guidance and different conditions.

EXTERNAL LEGITIMACY LAYER

Professional standards define “good.”

The Lab is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by these organizations. Their public frameworks inform how engagements are reviewed so the standard is inspectable outside the Lab.

Lens Engine

Each engagement activates only the standards that matter. A production AI system may trigger architecture, NIST AI RMF, OWASP, SRE, and FinOps. A product adoption problem may emphasize product, data, economics, and organizational execution instead.

Improvement over scoring

Reviews produce evidence and improvement actions, not decorative percentages. The question is what was demonstrated, what remains partial, and what must improve before the capability can be called independent.

EVIDENCE ENGINE

Claims require artifacts.

Capabilities advance through observed work: queries, analyses, designs, technical artifacts, decisions, incident handling, architecture reviews, financial models, and transfer to unfamiliar contexts.

JUST-IN-TIME SKILL CHAMBER

SQL: interrogating evidence

This is not a detour into a course. Learn only what you need to move the engagement forward, prove it in the workbench, then return to the client problem.

SELECT chooses the fields you want to inspect.
FROM identifies the table.
WHERE filters the evidence.
GROUP BY lets you compare cohorts or segments.
JOIN connects evidence that lives in different tables.
  • Start with a business question.
  • Write the smallest query that can answer it.
  • Inspect the result before adding complexity.
  • Do not claim causality from a descriptive query alone.
